  <big><strong>Review and Goals of the Third QFT Session</big></strong> <br>

  The session began with a review of the material covered in the first two quantum field theory sessions and established the goal of understanding the derivation and application of Feynman rules. The discussion revisited the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulations, creation and annihilation operators, and the treatment of time-dependent interaction terms in the interaction picture. <br>

  The calculation of two-to-two scattering processes was reviewed using transition matrix elements, cross-section formulas, and time-ordered products. Contour integration in the complex plane, including the use of Cauchy's theorem and related integration techniques, was also discussed. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Heaviside Functions and Time Ordering</big></strong> <br>

  The mathematical definition of the Heaviside step function and its role in time-ordered products were examined. It was noted that the particular convention adopted at the discontinuity generally does not affect the calculations under consideration. <br>

  The discussion then addressed the time-evolution operator in the interaction picture, where free fields evolve according to the free Hamiltonian and interactions are incorporated through the interaction Hamiltonian. The time interval relevant to the scattering operator was clarified as extending from the asymptotic past, t → −∞, to the asymptotic future, t → +∞. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Asymptotic States and the Infinite-Time Limit</big></strong> <br>

  The physical interpretation of the infinite-time limit in scattering theory was discussed. The statement that interactions vanish at infinite times was understood as the assumption that incoming and outgoing particles are sufficiently separated to behave as free asymptotic states, rather than as a literal claim that the fundamental interaction ceases to exist. <br>

  This assumption allows the interacting states to be related to well-defined free in-states and out-states and leads to the definition of the S-matrix as the time-evolution operator from the asymptotic past to the asymptotic future. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Wave Packets and Physical Scattering States</big></strong> <br>

  The role of wave packets in scattering theory was considered, particularly in distinguishing idealized plane-wave calculations from physically localized particles used in experiments. Wave packets provide a clearer description of particles that approach one another, interact within a finite region, and subsequently separate into outgoing states. <br>

  The discussion also examined how the formal theoretical description is connected to experimentally realizable scattering processes. Several conceptually difficult aspects of the construction were reviewed step by step. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Position-Space Feynman Rules</big></strong> <br>

  Position-space Feynman rules were introduced through examples of interacting fields. The construction of Feynman diagrams was explained in terms of vertices representing interaction terms and propagators connecting different spacetime points. <br>

  Further study was planned on the physical meaning and mathematical definition of the limits t → ±∞ and on the assumption of noninteracting asymptotic states. Any remaining conceptual questions concerning these assumptions were to be raised in a subsequent discussion. <br>
