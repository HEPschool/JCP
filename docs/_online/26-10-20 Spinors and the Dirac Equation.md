  <big><strong>Lorentz Transformations and Field Representations</big></strong> <br>

  The discussion reviewed the Lorentz transformation laws of scalar, vector, and spinor fields. <br> Particular attention was given to how these different representations allow Lorentz-invariant quantities to be constructed, even though the fields themselves generally transform nontrivially. <br>

  Spinor transformations were introduced as the basis for formulating a relativistic equation for spin-1/2 fields. <br> The relationship between these transformations and the Lorentz covariance of the Dirac equation was examined. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Clifford Algebra and Spinor Generators</big></strong> <br>

  Gamma matrices satisfying the Clifford algebra were used to construct the generators of Lorentz transformations in the spinor representation. <br> Their commutators provided the algebraic structure needed to describe rotations and boosts acting on spinor fields. <br>

  The compatibility relation between gamma matrices, spinor transformation matrices, and vector Lorentz transformations was examined to check the covariance of the Dirac operator. <br> Questions were raised about intermediate steps in this verification, and some details were left for further review. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Unitarity and the Dirac Lagrangian</big></strong> <br>

  The unitarity properties of Lorentz transformations were discussed, particularly the distinction between rotations and boosts in finite-dimensional spinor representations. <br> Boost matrices are generally nonunitary in this representation, prompting questions about their relationship to unitary representations acting on physical quantum states. <br>

  The Dirac Lagrangian was examined to verify that it transforms as a Lorentz scalar. <br> Applying the Euler–Lagrange equations then yielded the Dirac equation and connected the transformation properties of the fields with their equations of motion. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Chiral Decomposition and the Massless Limit</big></strong> <br>

  A Dirac spinor was decomposed into left-handed and right-handed components, and matrix representations suited to this decomposition were considered. <br> This approach made it possible to examine separately how the two chiral components transform under Lorentz transformations. <br>

  The role of the mass term in coupling the left-handed and right-handed components was discussed. <br> In the massless limit, the equations for these components decouple, leading to independent Weyl equations. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Helicity and Discrete Symmetries</big></strong> <br>

  Helicity was introduced alongside chirality to clarify the meaning of left-handed and right-handed descriptions. <br> The discussion emphasized the importance of particle mass when relating these concepts and interpreting the transformation of spinor states. <br>

  Examples involving CPT symmetry were also considered in connection with particles, antiparticles, and their handedness. <br> These examples highlighted the need to distinguish Lorentz transformation properties, chirality, and helicity when describing relativistic fermions. <br>
