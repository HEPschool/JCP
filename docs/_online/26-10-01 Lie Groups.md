  <big><strong>Lie Groups and Lie Algebras</big></strong> <br>

  The discussion introduced Lie groups and their associated Lie algebras. Lie groups were described as groups with a smooth manifold structure, while Lie algebras characterize their infinitesimal transformations through generators and commutation relations. <br>

  Structure constants were introduced as the coefficients expressing commutators in terms of the generators. Their consistency with the Jacobi identity was discussed, together with representations of the algebra by linear operators and the role of scalar products in representation spaces. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Lorentz Generators and Algebraic Structure</big></strong> <br>

  The construction and properties of the Lorentz-algebra generators were examined, including questions concerning boost generators and the assumptions imposed on the associated bilinear forms. The distinction between a vector with vanishing norm and the zero vector was considered. For a positive-definite inner product, vanishing norm implies that the vector itself is zero, whereas this implication does not generally hold for an indefinite form. <br>

  The distinction between simple and semisimple Lie algebras was also reviewed. Questions about the relevant algebraic conditions were considered alongside the Lorentz-algebra example, although some aspects of the discussion remained open. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Infinitesimal Transformations of States and Operators</big></strong> <br>

  Possible redundancies in the description of transformations were discussed. Particular attention was given to whether a parameter or degree of freedom could be eliminated and to the need to examine the complete set of generators before making such a reduction. <br>

  The discussion then turned to the transformations of states and operators under Lie-group actions. Infinitesimal transformations were used to connect the action of generators with commutator relations, and the Heisenberg equation of motion was considered as an example of how an operator evolves under a continuous transformation generated by the Hamiltonian. <br>
