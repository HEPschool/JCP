  <big><strong>Roots and Weights from SU(2) to SU(3)</big></strong><br>
  The discussion used the familiar SU(2) raising and lowering operators to introduce roots and weights. A weight recorded the simultaneous eigenvalues of the commuting Cartan generators. Roots were identified as the nonzero weights of the adjoint representation. Acting on a state of weight μ, the step operator associated with a root α either produced a state of weight μ + α or annihilated the state. <br>
  For SU(3), the two diagonal Gell-Mann matrices provided a basis for the Cartan subalgebra, so each weight could be plotted using two coordinates. The weight diagram showed the weights present in a chosen representation, while the root diagram showed the shifts associated with the step operators. Questions focused on how the algebra constrained the angles between roots. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Isospin and Flavor Multiplets</big></strong><br>
  The discussion connected the proton–neutron isospin doublet to the broader classification of baryons and mesons under approximate SU(3) flavor symmetry. A question about moving between points on a weight diagram led to a distinction between the action of a symmetry generator and the time evolution of a particle. Tensor products provided a related example: a composite flavor state could form a singlet, on which every flavor generator acted as zero. <br><br>
