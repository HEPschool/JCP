  <big><strong>Characters and Conjugacy Classes</big></strong> <br>

  The discussion began with the relationship between characters and traces of representation matrices. For a representation D, the character was defined by χ(g) = Tr[D(g)]. The cyclic property of the trace was then used to show that the character is invariant under conjugation and therefore remains constant throughout each conjugacy class. <br>

  Conjugacy classes in Abelian groups were considered as a simple example. Because every pair of elements commutes, conjugation leaves each element unchanged, so every element forms its own singleton conjugacy class. The regular representation was also reviewed in connection with character calculations. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Equivalent Representations and Class Functions</big></strong> <br>

  The basis independence of characters was discussed. Equivalent representations are related by similarity transformations and consequently have identical characters. Characters therefore depend on the equivalence class of a representation rather than on the particular basis used to express its matrices. <br>

  Characters were also examined as class functions, namely functions that are constant on conjugacy classes. The space of class functions and its possible spectral interpretation were considered, together with the role of irreducible characters as a natural basis for this function space. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Character Orthogonality and Multiplicities</big></strong> <br>

  Character orthogonality relations were reviewed as a method for analyzing representations. The inner product of characters can determine whether two irreducible representations are equivalent and can be used to calculate the multiplicity with which an irreducible representation appears in a reducible representation. <br>

  The discussion also considered how characters encode the sums of eigenvalues of representation matrices. Topological examples involving winding numbers were used to illustrate how invariant quantities can classify mathematical objects, while class functions provide the analogous conjugation-invariant quantities in representation theory. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Representations of the Permutation Group S3</big></strong> <br>

  The permutation group S3 was used as an explicit example for studying conjugacy classes, representations, and characters. The possibility of implementing these calculations computationally, for example in Python, was also discussed. <br>

  The trivial representation was identified as a one-dimensional representation. Since a one-dimensional vector space has no nontrivial proper subspaces, its only invariant subspaces are the zero subspace and the full space, and the representation is therefore irreducible. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Projection Operators and the Regular Representation</big></strong> <br>

  Projection operators were introduced as tools for extracting invariant subspaces and irreducible components from a representation. Their relationship to invariant subspaces and Schur's lemma was considered in the context of representation decomposition. <br>

  The regular representation of S3 was described using a basis labeled by the elements of the group. Group multiplication acts by permuting these basis vectors, producing a representation whose dimension equals the order of the group. Constructing explicit matrices was discussed as a way to realize the abstract group action on a vector space. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Tensor Products and Young Diagrams</big></strong> <br>

  The usefulness of character orthogonality for reducing computational work was considered. Although orthogonality relations provide a systematic method for identifying irreducible components and their multiplicities, they do not necessarily shorten every explicit calculation. <br>

  The final part of the discussion covered tensor-product representations, the cycle structure of permutation groups, and Young diagrams and Young tableaux. The hook-length formula was mentioned as a method for determining the dimensions of irreducible representations associated with Young diagrams, although its detailed derivation was left for later study. <br>
