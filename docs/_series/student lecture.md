**JCP HEP School: Student Lecture** is a recurring lecture series in which graduate students prepare and deliver introductory lectures on their own research fields. Each lecture brings together the physical motivation, core concepts, standard tools, and broader research landscape of a topic in high-energy physics or a related area.

Preparing a lecture gives speakers an opportunity to revisit the foundations of their research, while offering participants a starting point for exploring subjects beyond their immediate specialization.

## Learning from one another

Our aim is to deepen participants’ understanding of the concepts and methods underlying different research areas while fostering academic exchange and interaction among students from different research backgrounds and universities. Lectures help participants connect unfamiliar ideas to the physics they already know, follow discussions at conferences, and find starting points for further study and collaboration.

- Understand the main questions that motivate a research field and why they matter.
- Become familiar with its basic concepts, canonical models and commonly used methods.
- Learn how representative calculations, plots and physical results are obtained and interpreted.
- Become aware of open questions and discover useful references for further study.

## What to expect from a lecture

The Student Lecture series provides an opportunity for graduate students to introduce their own research fields to peers working on different topics in particle phenomenology and related areas. We aim to make each lecture accessible to participants who may be unfamiliar with the subject. To this end, we encourage speakers to provide sufficient background, explain key ideas and calculations step by step, and leave room for questions about definitions, assumptions, and intermediate steps.

We suggest the following general structure as a flexible guide for preparing lectures:

1. **Motivation and learning goals:** the physical problem, its place in high-energy physics, and what participants can expect to understand or calculate.
2. **Foundations:** the essential concepts, notation and background that support the discussion.
3. **Models and tools:** the theoretical frameworks and standard calculations used to study the problem.
4. **Representative results:** examples, figures and an overview of the field's current status.
5. **Questions for further study:** open problems and introductory papers or reviews that provide a path into the literature.

We encourage speakers to balance physical intuition with technical details, using figures and physical explanations to convey the broader picture and equations or worked examples to illustrate the underlying reasoning. Questions, including those about basic concepts, are welcome throughout the lecture.

## Format and materials

The series is organized as recurring workshop-style sessions, combining extended student-led lectures with opportunities for questions and discussion. Specific schedules and venues are announced for each event.

Lectures are generally delivered in Korean, while slides and lecture notes are prepared in English for later reference and sharing. Available materials, including supplementary resources, can be found on the [Materials page]({{ '/materials/' | relative_url }}).
