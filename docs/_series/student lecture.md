**JCP HEP School:  Student Lecture** is a recurring JCP HEP School program in which graduate students prepare and deliver introductory lectures on their own research fields. Each lecture brings together the physical motivation, core concepts, standard tools and broader research landscape of a topic in high-energy physics or a related area.

The series is built around learning from one another. Preparing a lecture gives the speaker an opportunity to revisit the foundations of their research, while participants gain a route into subjects beyond their immediate specialization.

## Learning from one another

Our aim is to build a shared language across research areas and universities. Lectures help participants connect unfamiliar ideas to the physics they already know, follow discussions at conferences, and find starting points for further study and collaboration.

- Understand the main questions that motivate a research field and why they matter.
- Become familiar with its basic concepts, canonical models and commonly used methods.
- Learn how representative calculations, plots and physical results are obtained and interpreted.
- Recognize current challenges and identify useful references for exploring the topic further.

## What to expect from a lecture

Lectures are designed for graduate students working on diverse research topics within high-energy physics and particle phenomenology. A speaker introduces the background needed to follow the subject and develops the material at a deliberate pace, with room to ask questions about definitions, assumptions and intermediate steps.

A typical lecture moves through:

1. **Motivation and learning goals:** the physical problem, its place in high-energy physics, and what participants can expect to understand or calculate.
2. **Foundations:** the essential concepts, notation and background that support the discussion.
3. **Models and tools:** the theoretical frameworks and standard calculations used to study the problem.
4. **Representative results:** examples, figures and an overview of the field's current status.
5. **Questions for further study:** open problems and introductory papers or reviews that provide a path into the literature.

Figures and physical explanations help establish the overall picture; equations and worked examples show how the underlying reasoning fits together. Questions are welcome throughout the lecture, including foundational ones.

## Format and materials

The series follows a monthly workshop format, with extended lectures and time for questions and discussion. It is intended primarily for graduate students in particle phenomenology and related fields who want to broaden their knowledge and exchange ideas with students at other universities.

Talks are primarily in Korean, with slides and notes prepared in English for later review and reuse. Available slides and notes can be found on the
