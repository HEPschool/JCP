  <big><strong>Schur Orthogonality Relations</big></strong> <br>

  The discussion began with the derivation of the orthogonality relations for irreducible group representations. An operator constructed by averaging linear transformations over all group elements was introduced, and Schur's lemma was used to analyze its properties. This procedure provided the basis for deriving the Schur orthogonality relations between matrix elements of inequivalent irreducible representations. <br>

  Characters were introduced as traces of representation matrices, and methods for calculating them were reviewed. The relationship between the regular representation and the space of functions on a group was also examined, with cyclic groups providing a simple example of how functions can be expanded in terms of representation matrix elements. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Generalization to Compact Continuous Groups</big></strong> <br>

  The discussion then considered how results for finite groups can be generalized to continuous groups. The distinction between finite groups and compact continuous groups, whose elements may have infinite order, was clarified. Compactness was identified as an important condition for defining a finite invariant measure and extending finite-group orthogonality relations to continuous groups. <br>

  The Peter–Weyl theorem was discussed as the continuous-group analogue of the decomposition of the regular representation for finite groups. According to this framework, square-integrable functions on a compact group can be expanded in terms of matrix elements of its irreducible unitary representations. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Rotations, Wigner D-Matrices, and Euler Angles</big></strong> <br>

  Rotations in SO(3) were examined using Euler angles and Wigner D-matrices. Euler angles provide a standard parameterization of three-dimensional rotations and allow both representation matrices and the invariant integration measure on SO(3) to be written explicitly. <br>

  The expansion of functions on SO(3) in terms of Wigner D-matrix elements was compared with the expansion of functions on the sphere in spherical harmonics. The relationship among SO(3), its double cover SU(2), and the three-sphere S³ was also considered as a related geometric and topological issue. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Haar Measure and Group Integration</big></strong> <br>

  The discussion reviewed the Haar measure, emphasizing that its definition is intrinsic to the group and does not depend on a particular parameterization. Explicit group-integration formulas for SO(2) and SO(3) were considered, together with the role of Euler angles in expressing the SO(3) measure. <br>

  Questions concerning dual operators or representations and the motivation behind the introduced formulas were also addressed. More generally, it was noted that mathematically elegant results may initially appear formal but often arise from specific structural or physical motivations. <br><br>

  <big><strong>Topology, Metrics, and Compactness of SO(3)</big></strong> <br>

  The distinction between topology and metric structure was discussed. A metric assigns distances between points and thereby induces a topology, whereas a topology more generally specifies notions such as open sets, continuity, and convergence without necessarily introducing a distance function. Compactness was considered as a topological property rather than a direct statement about distance. <br>

  SO(3) was described as the set of real orthogonal 3 × 3 matrices with determinant one. When embedded in the finite-dimensional Euclidean space of matrix components, this set is closed and bounded and is therefore compact. The determinant condition distinguishes SO(3) from the full orthogonal group O(3), while the surrounding Euclidean matrix space provides a convenient metric for analyzing its geometric properties. <br>
